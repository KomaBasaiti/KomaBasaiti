Polynomials — shape from the degree

A polynomial of degree n has at most n x-intercepts and at most n − 1 turning points. Only the leading term decides where the arms go:

FactorPicture near x = aWhat the graph doesSign changes?
(x − a)cuts straight throughyes
(x − a)²touches and turns back (turning point)no
(x − a)³flattens then goes through (stationary point of inflection)yes

Rule of thumb: even power → touch, odd power → cross.

Exampley = (x − 1)²(x + 3): touches the x-axis at x = 1, cuts at x = −3. It is a cubic but has only 2 distinct zeros.

TRAP

Double factor touches, triple factor flattens

At (x − a)² the curve turns back. At (x − a)³ it flattens and keeps going. Students often draw a turning point at a triple factor — don't.
