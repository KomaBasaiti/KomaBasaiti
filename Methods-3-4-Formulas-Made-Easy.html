Polynomials — shape from the degree

A polynomial of degree n has at most n x-intercepts and at most n − 1 turning points. Only the leading term decides where the arms go:

FactorPicture near x = aWhat the graph doesSign changes?
(x − a)cuts straight throughyes
(x − a)²touches and turns back (turning point)no
(x − a)³flattens then goes through (stationary point of inflection)yes

Rule of thumb: even power → touch, odd power → cross.

Exampley = (x − 1)²(x + 3): touches the x-axis at x = 1, cuts at x = −3. It is a cubic but has only 2 distinct zeros.

Use the discriminant — do not solve

"For what k is the line a tangent?" → set the rules equal, collect into ax² + bx + c = 0, then put Δ = 0. (Δ > 0: two intersections, Δ < 0: none.) No need to actually solve the quadratic.

Split the numerator instead of long division

Make the top contain the bottom: (3x + 1)/(x − 2) = (3(x − 2) + 7)/(x − 2) = 3 + 7/(x − 2). Now you can read the asymptotes straight off: x = 2 and y = 3.

CHECK

Endpoint or asymptote? Pick the right bracket

A square-root endpoint is reached → square bracket: range of y = −2√(x + 1) + 3 is (−∞, 3]. An asymptote is never reached → round bracket / "\": range of the truncus above is (3, ∞).

Double factor touches, triple factor flattens

At (x − a)² the curve turns back. At (x − a)³ it flattens and keeps going. Students often draw a turning point at a triple factor — don't.

Step 1: ran g = [−1, 3].

TRAP

It is ran g ⊆ dom f, not dom g ⊆ dom f

The test is on the outputs of the inside function. Write down ran g, write down dom f, then compare those two sets.

TRAP

Mutually exclusive is NOT the same as independent

Mutually exclusive: A and B can't both happen (Pr(A ∩ B) = 0). Independent: one doesn't change the chance of the other. If both events can happen on their own, mutually exclusive events are always dependent. Always prove independence with numbers: compare Pr(A ∩ B) with Pr(A) × Pr(B).

E(X) is the "mean from a frequency table" you already know

In Year 7: mean = Σ(score × frequency) ÷ total. Divide each frequency by the total and you get a probability — so the mean becomes E(X) = Σ x · Pr(X = x).

TRAP

Off by one in "at least"

For a discrete X: Pr(X ≥ 3) = 1 − Pr(X ≤ 2), not 1 − Pr(X ≤ 3). For Bi(20, 0.4) the right answer is 0.9964; the wrong one gives 0.9840 — close enough to look fine, still wrong.

TRICK

"At least one" is one line

Never add Pr(X = 1) + Pr(X = 2) + … Use 1 − (1 − p)ⁿ. With n = 5, p = 0.2: 1 − 0.8⁵ = 0.67232.
Backwards — "how many trials for at least a 95% chance?": 1 − 0.8ⁿ ≥ 0.95 → n ≥ 13.4 → n = 14 (always round up; n = 13 only gives 0.945).

TRICK

Conditional binomial: divide, don't subtract

Pr(X ≥ 2 | X ≥ 1) = Pr(X ≥ 2) ÷ Pr(X ≥ 1) because "X ≥ 2" sits inside "X ≥ 1". For Bi(8, 0.25): 0.6329 ÷ 0.8999 = 0.7033.

CHECK

Binomial variance is always smaller than the mean

np(1 − p) < np. Bi(20, 0.4): mean 8, variance 4.8. If you get a variance of 8 or more, you forgot the (1 − p).

FactWhat to remember
Shapebell-shaped, symmetric about μ → mean = median = mode = μ

TRICK

Use symmetry — three answers from one calculation

65 and 75 are both 5 (= 1.25σ) away from 70. So once you know Pr(X > 75) = 0.1056, you also know Pr(X < 65) = 0.1056 and Pr(65 < X < 75) = 1 − 2(0.1056) = 0.7887.

Example — find an unknown μσ = 5 and Pr(X < 50) = 0.025. Find μ.
① z = invNorm(0.025) = −1.96   ② 50 = μ + (−1.96)(5)   ③ μ = 50 + 9.8 = 59.8.
Sense check: a small left-tail probability means 50 is below μ ✓
TRAP

The calculator wants σ, not σ²

X ~ N(50, 9): type σ = 3. Write "σ = …" on the page before you touch the calculator.

TRAP

Standard deviations don't add — variances do

For two independent X₁, X₂ with sd σ, Var(X₁ + X₂) = 2σ², so sd = σ√2. But 2X (one value doubled) has sd 2σ.

TRAP

Round the sample size UP — always

n ≥ 1067.1 means 1068, even though .1 would normally round down. n = 1067 gives M = 0.030001, which is too big.

TRAP

Say what a confidence interval means — correctly

✓ Correct"We are 95% confident that the interval (0.304, 0.496) contains p." Or: "about 95% of intervals made this way would contain p."
✗ Wrong"There is a 95% chance that p is between 0.304 and 0.496." — p is a fixed number; it is the interval that changes between samples.
CHECK

Three checks on any interval

① p̂ is exactly the middle. ② Both ends are between 0 and 1. ③ On the same data, 99% is wider than 95%, which is wider than 90%.
